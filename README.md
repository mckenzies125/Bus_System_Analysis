This project analyzes Off Campus College Transport, Inc. (OCCT) ridership data to identify patterns in student transportation behavior and evaluate possible service improvements. Using Tableau visualizations, inferred travel patterns, and negative binomial regression models, the report examines system-wide ridership trends, Campus Shuttle efficiency, bidirectional shuttle feasibility, and the relationship between the Main Street and Westside corridors.

The analysis shows that OCCT ridership is highly concentrated around major routes, central campus stops, and weekday daytime/evening travel periods. University Union functions as the dominant system hub, while stops such as Mohawk, Engineering Building, Hillside, UCLUB, and University Downtown Center also show substantial activity. The reverse Campus Shuttle analysis suggests that bidirectional demand exists for certain stop pairs, but not evenly across the entire route. The Main Street and Westside corridor analysis finds that Westside fullness is positively associated with expected Main Street boardings in both directions, suggesting that Main Street may function as a complementary corridor during periods of higher Westside demand. Overall, the findings suggest that future OCCT planning should consider route direction, stop-level demand, time of day, bus fullness, and corridor interactions rather than relying only on aggregate ridership totals.
